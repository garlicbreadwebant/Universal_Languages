# Narela grammar 0.1

The following rules preserve the original starter grammar. Narela and Lumena have different vocabularies and rules; use one language consistently.

## Core rules

1. Word order: subject + verb + object. Mi seva ti = I see you.
2. Verbs never change for person or number. Present is unmarked. Put pa before a verb for past, fu for future, and du for ongoing action.
3. Negation: no immediately before the verb. Mi no sava = I do not know. Marker order: subject + tense + du + no + verb + object; omit unused markers.
4. Questions: add ka at the end for yes/no questions. Ti sava ka? = Do you know? Otherwise put the question word where its answer belongs: Ti seva ke? = What do you see?
5. Adjectives follow nouns: doma bala = big house. Adjectives never change for plural.
6. Plural nouns add -n: buki = book; bukin = books. After a number the plural ending is optional.
7. Possession: thing + de + owner. Buki de mi = my book. Pronouns have the same subject and object forms.
8. Articles: un = a/an; la = the. Articles can be omitted when the meaning is clear.
9. Commands use the bare verb: Vena! = Come! Add pali for please.
10. Use esa for be and exi for exist: Mi esa hapi = I am happy. Exi un doma = There is a house.
11. Can and must precede the main verb: Mi pote reda = I can read. Mi deve vada = I must go. Negation precedes the modal: Mi no pote reda = I cannot read.
12. Comparison: plu + adjective = more; maxi + adjective = most. Plu bala = bigger.
13. Numbers precede nouns. Adverbs precede the main verb after any grammar markers.
14. Optional word building: verb + -ri names its doer (lena, learn; lenari, learner). Noun + -la makes a related adjective (sola, sun; solala, solar).

## Numbers

Build numbers largest unit first. A multiplier precedes its unit: des en = 11; do des = 20; do des tri = 23; en sent pen des ses = 156. Tri buki = three books.

## Precision and scope

Use a full affirmative or negative clause when answering a negative question. The original starter does not define a special answer convention for negative questions.

The given modal pattern distinguishes inability from lack of obligation: Mi no pote vada = I cannot go; Mi no deve vada = I do not have to go. A separate construction for must not is not standardized. Rephrase as a negative command where suitable.

The starter does not standardize relative clauses, nested reported speech, passive constructions, or recursive large-number expressions. Prefer short independent clauses until these are defined and reviewed. This edition documents those limits instead of importing Lumena rules.

## Adding words

For each new entry, record its English meaning, Narela spelling, part of speech, and an example. Check existing roots before inventing another. Prefer one clear everyday meaning per root and explicitly note deliberate shared meanings. Keep pronunciation and grammar consistent. Future additions can cover occupations, technology, detailed emotions, and specialized topics.

Edit lexicon.csv and run the checker with --write to update both dictionaries. New specialized senses need explicit notes and usage examples.
