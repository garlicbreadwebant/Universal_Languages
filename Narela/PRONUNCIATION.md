# Narela pronunciation

## Starter pronunciation

Pronounce every letter. Vowels: a as in father, e as in bed, i as in machine, o as in go, u as in flute. Keep each vowel steady. Consonants have their usual English sounds; g is always hard as in give. Stress the next-to-last syllable. Adjacent vowels are separate sounds.

## Reference sounds

This table makes the original English-based guidance more explicit. The entries for x, y, r and h clarify previously underspecified letters; they are conventions for this reference edition, not changes to vocabulary.

| Letters | Reference IPA | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| a e i o u | /a e i o u/ | Five steady vowels; avoid an English-style glide in e and o |
| b d g | /b d g/ | Voiced stops; g is always hard |
| p t k | /p t k/ | Unvoiced stops |
| f v | /f v/ | Lower lip near upper teeth |
| s z | /s z/ | As in see and zoo |
| m n | /m n/ | Nasal consonants |
| l | /l/ | As in light |
| r | /ɾ/ | A short tongue tap; an understandable trill or approximant is acceptable |
| h | /h/ | Audible breath as in hat |
| x | /ks/ | Two sounds, as in exi and maxi |
| y | /j/ | As in yes |
| w | /w/ | As in we; used in wata |

Narela uses consonant clusters and final consonants: klosa, stana, tri, nul, and the plural ending -n. Do not replace these with Lumena's open-syllable rules. Adjacent vowels have separate syllables. Stress the next-to-last syllable; stress the only syllable in a one-syllable word. Adding plural -n does not add a syllable.

The letter sequence th in hotha is /t.h/, with each letter pronounced; it is not the English sound in thin. Preferred syllabification is hot-ha. English pronunciation hints remain approximate across accents.

## Names and writing

Capitalization does not change meaning. Preserve people's preferred names and ask for their pronunciation. Use spaces between words and ordinary punctuation. IPA is a pronunciation reference, not additional spelling to memorize. Audio demonstrations and learner studies remain future work.
