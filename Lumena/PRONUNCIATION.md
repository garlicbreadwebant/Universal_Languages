# Pronunciation and spelling

## Alphabet

Use these 14 lowercase Latin letters: **a e i k l m n o p s t u w y**. Capitalization has no grammatical meaning. Native vocabulary has one sound per letter and no silent letters or digraphs. IPA below is the reference; English hints are approximate and depend on accent.

| Letter | IPA | Articulation or approximate hint |
|---|---|---|
| a | /a/ | Open central vowel; approximately the vowel in Spanish casa |
| e | /e/ | Mid front vowel; a steady vowel, without the glide in English day |
| i | /i/ | High front vowel; approximately machine |
| o | /o/ | Mid back rounded vowel; steady, without the glide in English go |
| u | /u/ | High back rounded vowel; approximately flute |
| p | /p/ | Lips close and release; approximately spin's p |
| t | /t/ | Tongue at teeth or ridge behind teeth |
| k | /k/ | Back of tongue closes against the soft palate |
| m | /m/ | Lips closed, sound through nose |
| n | /n/ | Tongue at teeth or ridge, sound through nose |
| s | /s/ | Approximately see |
| l | /l/ | Tongue touches ridge; air passes along its sides |
| w | /w/ | Approximately we |
| y | /j/ | Approximately yes; always a consonant |

## Syllables and stress

Each syllable is a vowel alone or one consonant plus one vowel: **(C)V**. Repeat this pattern to form words. `aku` divides `a-ku`; `Lumena` divides `lu-me-na`. Adjacent vowels remain separate syllables: `mea` is `me-a`. There are no diphthongs in the reference pronunciation.

Stress the next-to-last syllable of each word; a one-syllable word has its only syllable. `lena` is **LE**-na, `Lumena` is lu-**ME**-na. In connected speech, particles may be lighter, but do not drop their vowels. Maintain word boundaries, particularly between successive vowels.

Slight aspiration, dental versus alveolar t, and modest differences in vowel quality are acceptable when understandable. Aim for the reference sounds; no rule requires an English accent. There is no competing r sound, but l remains the spelling and reference articulation. Accent tolerance does not guarantee that every substitution is intelligible.

## Names, writing, and access

Keep personal and place names in their owners' preferred spelling. For speech, ask the person for a pronunciation; optionally record a consented Lumena approximation alongside the original. Foreign names and quotations are outside native phonotactic checks. Do not silently rename people.

Spaces separate words. Use ordinary periods, commas, and question marks; punctuation is a reading aid, not a replacement for spoken grammatical markers. Arabic digits are accepted. Hyphens join transparent lexical compounds in writing; pronounce both components as separate words with their own stress. A compound containing a grammatical particle is not a native lexical compound.

The Latin alphabet reduces typing requirements on many systems but is not equally familiar worldwide. Teaching should include sound demonstrations and later script-specific transliteration guides. This edition provides text and IPA, not validated audio, Braille conventions, or a signed equivalent. Do not describe it as accessible to everyone merely because its rules are regular.
