# First lessons in Taluma

Use the [pronunciation guide](PRONUNCIATION.md) first. Say short phrases clearly and check the English meaning before adding more grammar. No fixed study duration guarantees fluency.

## 1. Meet someone

| Taluma | English |
|---|---|
| salo. | Hello. |
| mi esa Ana. | I am Ana. |
| nomo te ti esa ke? | What is your name? |
| tani. | Thank you. |
| sawawe. | You are welcome. |
| pali sapu mi. | Please help me. |
| mi no kowa. | I do not understand. |
| pali tala senu-me. | Please speak slowly. |

Ana is a personal name. Names keep their owners' spellings. Start with full affirmative or negative answers when a short yes or no might confuse someone.

## 2. Choose and describe

| Taluma | English |
|---|---|
| mi leka sa lipo. | I read an unspecified book. |
| mi leka la lipo. | I read the identifiable book. |
| mi awa tuli lipo pona. | I have two good books. |
| la lipo esa sula tapula. | The book is on the table. |
| mi sewa nila yapa. | I see a fast child. |
| mi sewa nila yapa-me. | I see the child quickly. |

Sa and la are real article words. Bare lipo can be book or books; use una or ma when exact singular/plural information matters. Descriptions follow nouns; a manner adverb follows the object and uses -me.

## 3. Actions and time

| Taluma | English |
|---|---|
| mi lena Taluma. | I learn Taluma. |
| mi tu lena Taluma. | I am learning Taluma. |
| mu pa pala a tomo pati. | We walked home yesterday. |
| lu pu wena puti. | They will come tomorrow. |
| ti sipa aku ka? | Do you drink water? |
| mi numa nami. | I eat food. |
| mi leka lipo kasi-me. | I read a book carefully. |

Unmarked time is present unless a time phrase in that same clause says otherwise. Repeat a tense marker in a new clause when necessary. Tomo identifies a dwelling; add te mi to mean mine.

## 4. Build useful words

| Taluma | English |
|---|---|
| lena-ki | learner / student |
| nisa-ki | teacher |
| lena-mo | school / learning place |
| siki-po | writing tool |
| sola-la | solar |
| sana-se | health |
| kasi-me | carefully |

The six suffixes keep their meanings. A place for learning is not automatically an accredited institution. Further specialized meanings need their own usage notes.

## 5. Explain clearly

| Taluma | English |
|---|---|
| mi sawa kepi ti pa wena nasuku. | I know that you came. |
| penu yali ye sapu mi moteka esa na tomo. | The person who helps me is in the house. |
| si ti wena nopaku mi pu pami nami. | If you come, I will make food. |
| mi no tewa pala. | I am not obliged to walk. |
| mi tewa kepi mi no pala nasuku. | I am obliged not to walk. |
| li teli kotu mi wola aku pusano. | They say: I want water. |

Speak each closing word. Repeat names or nouns if two people could both be li. Prefer two short sentences when nesting becomes hard to follow.

## Practice and answers

Translate before checking:

1. I do not see you.
2. Will they come?
3. My two books.
4. Only I learn.
5. I also learn.
6. I cannot walk.
7. Please do not go.
8. I know that you are learning.

Answers:

1. `mi no sewa ti.`
2. `lu pu wena ka?`
3. `tuli lipo te mi`
4. `solo mi lena.`
5. `mi lena aniki.`
6. `mi no pote pala.`
7. `pali no wata!`
8. `mi sawa kepi ti tu lena nasuku.`

Practice repairing misunderstandings: mi no kowa (I do not understand), ke? (what?), and pali lati (please repeat). Ask which person or thing is intended and repeat it explicitly. Conversation and delayed recall tests are needed to assess whether these rules work well for diverse learners.
