# Taluma pronunciation

## Alphabet and sounds

Use 14 letters: a e i k l m n o p s t u w y. Each has one reference sound. There are no silent letters, consonant clusters, final consonants, lexical tones, or meaningful vowel-length contrasts in native words.

| Letters | Reference IPA | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| a e i o u | /a e i o u/ | Five steady vowels; keep e and o free of a trailing glide |
| p t k | /p t k/ | Unvoiced stops; no voiced/unvoiced contrast to memorize |
| m n | /m n/ | Nasals |
| s | /s/ | As in see |
| l | /l/ | As in light; there is no separate r phoneme |
| w | /w/ | As in we |
| y | /j/ | As in yes; always a consonant |

English hints are approximate. Learners should use the IPA and articulatory descriptions with a teacher where possible. T is made with the tongue at the teeth or ridge behind them; k uses the back of the tongue. Vowel and consonant categories may still be unfamiliar to some learners.

## Syllables, stress, and boundaries

Each syllable is a vowel alone or one consonant plus one vowel: (C)V. Adjacent vowels make separate syllables. Stress the next-to-last syllable of the complete word: ta-LU-ma, LE-na, le-NA-ki. Stress the only syllable of a one-syllable word. A derivational suffix becomes part of the spoken word even though a hyphen makes its boundary visible in writing.

Do not drop vowels or merge words when speaking carefully. Pause at clause boundaries and preserve complete marker words. Kepi opens a content clause and nasuku closes it; yali opens a relative clause and moteka closes it. Their different lengths and consonants avoid relying on a single final-vowel distinction, but this still needs listening tests.

Ordinary lexical compounds keep a boundary and each component's own stress. Thus lipo-nuti (newspaper) is LI-po NU-ti; lena-ki (learner) is le-NA-ki. The closed suffix list in the grammar distinguishes derivation from a lexical compound.

## Names and access

Keep people's preferred names and ask how to pronounce them. Taluma and Esperanto are language names; Ana is a lesson name. Borrowed names may fall outside native spelling rules. Do not require people to rename themselves.

Accent variation is expected; do not promise that every substitution preserves intelligibility. Recordings, listening trials, multilingual teaching, accessible formats, and community review remain necessary. Browser and AI voices are approximations rather than pronunciation authorities.
