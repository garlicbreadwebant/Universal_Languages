# Esperanto collection design and sources

## Scope

Esperanto is an established international auxiliary language. Lumena and Narela are separate project prototypes. This folder adds learning material for Esperanto without changing its spelling, vocabulary, or grammar to match either prototype.

The folder follows the same project structure: six reference guides, a CSV lexicon, two generated dictionaries, and a Node.js validation tool. The website reuses the library's paper-and-green theme, navigation, dictionary, flashcards, and optional AI tools.

## Editorial choices

Entries use Unicode spelling and infinitive verbs. English labels distinguish senses where necessary. Notes explain usage and avoid treating a short English gloss as a full definition. Lessons introduce agreement and object endings early, so learners do not mistake Esperanto for an uninflected language.

This is a curated starter vocabulary, not the complete Esperanto lexicon. Edition numbers and changelog entries refer only to these materials. Automated checks catch malformed data and stale dictionaries; they cannot establish linguistic correctness or teaching quality.

## Sources and further study

- [Fundamento de Esperanto](https://www.akademio-de-esperanto.org/fundamento/index.html): foundational grammar, exercises, and vocabulary published by the Akademio de Esperanto.
- [Fundamento grammar in English](https://www.akademio-de-esperanto.org/fundamento/gramatiko_angla.html): reference for the alphabet and basic grammatical rules.
- [Plena Manlibro de Esperanta Gramatiko](https://bertilow.com/pmeg/): Bertilo Wennergren's detailed guide to contemporary usage.
- [PMEG pronunciation](https://bertilow.com/pmeg/skribo_elparolo/elparolo/index.html): sound and stress guidance.

The explanations, lesson sentences, and concise English usage notes here are original learning material, rather than reproduced passages from these references. Follow the linked works for their complete explanations.

## Review and contribution

Report errors with a reference and a corrected example. Improve this collection's explanations rather than proposing language-wide reforms. Test pronunciation, agreement, object marking, Unicode search, and sentence examples with learners from different language backgrounds. Do not promise equal learning difficulty for everyone.
