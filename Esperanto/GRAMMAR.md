# Esperanto grammar

This is a beginner summary of established usage. It does not redefine Esperanto. See [design and sources](DESIGN.md) for further study.

## Nouns and the article

Nouns end in -o. Add -j for the plural and -n for a direct object, in that order: libro (book), libroj (books), libron (book as object), librojn (books as object). The definite article la means the and does not change. There is no indefinite article corresponding to a or an.

`Mi legas libron.` — I read a book.

`Mi legas la librojn.` — I read the books.

## Adjectives and adverbs

Adjectives end in -a and agree with their noun in number and case. Derived adverbs usually end in -e. Some common adverbs such as nun and tre have other forms.

`La bonaj amikoj legas bonajn librojn.` — The good friends read good books.

`Ŝi parolas klare.` — She speaks clearly.

After esti (to be), a predicate adjective has no object ending: `La libro estas bona.` — The book is good.

## Pronouns

| Esperanto | English |
|---|---|
| mi | I |
| vi | you; singular or plural |
| li | he |
| ŝi | she |
| ĝi | it |
| ni | we |
| ili | they |
| si | reflexive referring to a third-person subject |
| oni | people in general; one |

Add -n for objects and -a for possessive adjectives: min (me), vin (you as object), mia (my), via (your). Possessives agree like other adjectives: miaj libroj (my books). Si cannot replace mi, vi, or ni.

## Verbs

Verbs do not change for person or number. Dictionary entries use the infinitive ending -i.

| Ending | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -i | infinitive | lerni — to learn |
| -as | present | lernas — learns / is learning |
| -is | past | lernis — learned |
| -os | future | lernos — will learn |
| -us | conditional | lernus — would learn |
| -u | command or wish | lernu — learn! |

`Ni lernis hieraŭ.` — We learned yesterday.

`Ili lernos morgaŭ.` — They will learn tomorrow.

`Mi volas lerni.` — I want to learn.

## Questions and negation

Start a yes/no question with ĉu. Place ne before the expression being negated; before the verb it normally negates the statement.

`Ĉu vi parolas Esperanton?` — Do you speak Esperanto?

`Mi ne komprenas.` — I do not understand.

`Kie estas la domo?` — Where is the house?

## Word order and prepositions

Start with subject–verb–object. The object ending allows other orders, but beginners need not rearrange sentences. Ordinary prepositions govern a noun without -n: en la domo (in the house), kun amiko (with a friend). Direction into a place can use -n: en la domon (into the house). The -n ending also has uses for measures and time expressions; it is not exclusively an object marker.

## Numbers and word building

Numbers do not agree like adjectives: unu libro (one book), du libroj (two books), dek du libroj (twelve books). Compound numbers combine hundreds, tens, and units.

Roots combine with productive affixes: mal- forms an opposite (bona → malbona); -et- makes something small (domo → dometo); -ej- indicates a place (lerni → lernejo). These are examples, not a license to assume every possible compound has a conventional meaning.

## Beyond this guide

The shared English vocabulary includes grammar labels from other languages. Some Esperanto entries are endings (-j or -is), worked constructions (estas leganta, is reading; estas leginta, has read), or names of concepts with no equivalent spoken marker. The active participle agrees with the subject in number; its relative time is measured against the tense of esti. Ordinary simple tenses often suffice. Read each entry's notes before using it as a translation. Slash-separated forms are alternatives, such as bona / bone for good / well.

Participles, correlatives, subordinate clauses, transitivity, and nuanced affix use deserve further study. Consult [PMEG](https://bertilow.com/pmeg/) and the [Fundamento](https://www.akademio-de-esperanto.org/fundamento/index.html). The starter dictionary is intentionally limited.
